Gold never cleans

Lesson 6 · Thursday 09:45 · Two tables shaped for two questions, and drop 3 fixed where it belongs

≈ 2 hours 30Prerequisite: lesson 5, drop 3 through silverReference: PySpark & Delta cheat sheet · glossary
Why this lesson Gold is the only layer the client sees, so it's tempting to "just fix it in the query". But that fixes one chart and leaves silver wrong for every other reader. Today you build two gold tables, then fix the drop-3 numbers in silver and watch gold fix itself.

1. One table, one question

Gold tableQuestionGrain (one row per…)
nutriscore_by_categoryHow do Nutri-Score grades spread across categories, per country?category × country × grade, with a product count
brands_sugarWhich brands have the most products, and how sugary are they?brand, top 10 by product count, with average sugars_100g

You rebuild both from silver every run and write them with mode("overwrite"). Why not MERGE? Gold is small and there's nothing to merge. At a client's GB a day, rebuilding gold from all of silver every run gets slow and costly. That's when gold moves to partitions or a MERGE.

categories and countries hold comma-separated lists, so the first table needs one row per element: F.explode of F.split. That's reshaping, gold's job: no value changes.

Try it in a scratch script. Predict the row count first.

spark.createDataFrame([("France,Spain",)], ["countries"]).select(F.explode(F.split("countries", ",")).alias("country")).show()

4. The temptation, and where the fix goes core

Drop 3 sent a lot of rows to quarantine yesterday, so they're missing from brands_sugar. Check your gold numbers: some brands' counts are low.

You could patch gold.py to read bronze and parse "12 g" there. Don't. Gold reads silver only, and a value gold would have to fix is wrong in silver too, for every other reader.

Gold reshapes, it never repairs Does a gold query need a trim, a cast, a parse or a filter for bad rows? Then that line belongs in silver (glossary: gold). Gold can reshape (explode, group, join, rank). It can't repair values.

So extend split in silver. Each change gets a row in your lesson-4 test first:

  • A quantity with a unit: strip a trailing g before the cast. F.expr(r"try_cast(regexp_replace(trim(sugars_100g), '\\s*g$', '') AS double)") turns "12 g" into 12.0 and leaves "lots" as null.
  • A second date format: F.coalesce of two try_to_timestamp parses, yyyy-MM-dd first, then dd/MM/yyyy (datetime patterns).
# add to test_each_rejected_row_names_its_rule, and expect them in good:
("006", "unit",   "12 g", "2026-03-01"),   # -> ("006", 12.0)
("007", "frDate", "0",    "15/03/2026"),   # -> ("007", 0.0), last_modified 2026-03-15
